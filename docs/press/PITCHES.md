## Facts you can use (and the ones you can't)

Every pitch sticks to these. They come from the "Safe to say" list in
`jason-voice/posts/2026-10-instagram-predatory-pricing.md`, the README and the threat model.

**Safe to say**
- Nullecho is free and open source (MIT), needs no account, shows no ads, and doesn't send your data anywhere.
- It blocks known trackers (ad networks, analytics, social pixels, fingerprinting companies).
- It sends Global Privacy Control, a "do not sell or share my data" signal that California, Colorado and several
  other states legally recognize (for their residents).
- It shows each site a different, realistic device profile, so **most** trackers can't link your visits.
- It's live for Chrome on the Chrome Web Store (since October 5, 2026). Firefox, Edge and Safari: "coming soon",
  no dates.
- New York has required a pricing disclosure since Nov 10, 2025. Maryland's ban (large food retailers and third-party
  food delivery) began Oct 1, 2026. Connecticut takes effect July 1, 2027. New Jersey's grocery ban by Aug 1, 2027.
- Maryland's § 13-322 warning label was struck before the bill passed; Maryland's site shows "File Not Found" for it.
- Your FTC comment is public: **FTC-2026-1057-1137**, posted Sept 21, 2026, on regulations.gov.

**Say these limits when it fits** (reporters trust a tool more when it says where it fails)
- A site can tell Nullecho is installed.
- A tracker that spots faked values and throws them out can still link visits (CreepJS does this).
- The canvas protection can be beaten: a page that draws the canvas about 21 times and takes a majority vote gets
  the real one back (documented Oct 14, 2026). No fix shipped.
- Nothing about your IP address. Firefox's built-in protection and Brave are stronger.
- It can't get anyone a better price.

**Never say**
- That it gets anyone a better price, or that it ends surveillance pricing.
- That it blocks every tracker, hides you completely, or makes you anonymous.
- That a named company broke the law.
- Your FTC **tracking** number. Only the public comment ID above.
- That Firefox, Edge or Safari can be installed before its store listing is live.
- That websites charge Mac users more.
