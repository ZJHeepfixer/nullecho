DROP

California runs a free state platform that makes registered data brokers delete what they hold on you. One request reaches all of them.

This is the only thing in Nullecho that removes data. Everything else here degrades tracking or adds noise — this causes actual deletion, by law, with a fine attached.

1 Are you a California resident?

DROP is open to California residents only. That is a statutory limit, not our choice.

CalPrivacy says you are a resident if either applies:

  • You live in California
  • You are domiciled in California but outside it for a temporary or transitory purpose

Factors that help determine residency:

  • Where your primary residence is
  • Which state issued your driver's licence or ID
  • Where you are registered to vote

Note the second bullet: some people who currently live elsewhere still qualify, if California is still their domicile. A former resident who has genuinely moved and re-domiciled does not. If you are unsure, read CalPrivacy's own residency guidance — we are not able to make that call for you.

You're outside California

DROP genuinely is California-only, so we won't pretend otherwise. But the useful part of your situation is that the brokers are the same companies — California just gave its residents a single lever to pull. Yours are individual levers. They still work.

1. Check whether your state has a deletion right

As of 2026, 20 states have comprehensive consumer privacy laws: California, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, Indiana, Iowa, Kentucky, Maryland, Minnesota, Montana, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Jersey, Oregon, Rhode Island, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, Virginia and Washington.

Most of these include a right to request deletion, but the scope, the exemptions and who counts as a covered business differ by state — so check yours rather than assuming. The list above moves; the tracker below is the thing to trust.

Texas, Oregon and Vermont also run data broker registries, but none of them has a centralized deletion mechanism — you identify brokers from the registry and submit to each one individually. Vermont's registry is the oldest and is publicly searchable, but Vermont has no companion law granting deletion rights.

2. Turn on Global Privacy Control

GPC is a signal Nullecho sends with every request that legally means "do not sell or share my data" in states that recognise it. It is not a deletion — it is a stop-selling instruction — and enforcement is real but partial: a 2025 USENIX Security study found only about a third of sites that appear to sell data implement any opt-out signal, and only 44% of those honoured all of them. Worth having on. Not worth over-trusting.

3. Use the blocking and fingerprint defence

Deletion removes what has already been collected. Nullecho's network blocking and per-origin personas reduce what gets collected next. Those work regardless of where you live, and they are the part of this extension that runs every day.

4. Broker-by-broker opt-outs

Slow and manual, but not futile — many brokers accept opt-out requests from anyone, regardless of state. Privacy Rights Clearinghouse maintains a non-commercial directory with the opt-out link for each broker.

2 What DROP actually does — and doesn't

What it does

  • Sends one request to every data broker registered in California — 603 of them in our snapshot.
  • Requires them to delete matching personal information including inferences — the profile they built about you, not just the raw fields.
  • Is ongoing, not one-time. After the initial pass, brokers must re-check and delete newly matching data at least every 45 days. You do not resubmit.
  • Is free. CalPrivacy: "we will never charge you to use DROP."
  • Has teeth: $200 per deletion request, per day a broker fails to delete (Civ. Code § 1798.99.82).

What it doesn't

  • California residents only. No exceptions, no workaround.
  • Covers registered data brokers only — not every company holding your data. Your bank, your employer, your doctor and the apps you signed up for yourself are not data brokers and are untouched by this.
  • Nullecho cannot submit for you. The state verifies your identity; that is the whole point, and it is not something an extension can or should do on your behalf.
  • Some brokers may lawfully keep data under statutory exemptions — you'll see those as "Exempted".
  • "Opted-out" is not deletion. It means the broker couldn't match you exactly, so it still holds your data but may no longer sell or share it.

Nullecho stores nothing about you. Nothing on this page is transmitted anywhere. The broker list below is a snapshot bundled with the extension, so browsing it makes no network request and tells no one you were looking. The only thing we ever write is a date you choose to record, in local extension storage, on this machine.

3 Have this ready before you start

You can complete a request with only the three required items. Everything else improves the odds a broker can match you.

Required

  • Name
  • Date of birth
  • ZIP code

That's the whole minimum. "You only need to provide your name, date of birth, and ZIP code to submit a request."

Optional — but each one widens the net

  • Email addresses
  • Phone numbers
  • Maiden / former names
  • Mobile advertising ID (MAID)
  • Connected TV ID
  • Vehicle identification number (VIN)

You can enter multiples of everything except date of birth. The last three take a minute to look up — find them first or add them later. How to find them ↗

On identity verification. Residency is confirmed through the California Identity Gateway, the state's digital identity platform. Per CalPrivacy you do not need to create an account, and your information is not retained by DROP. You may either enter your information directly or sign in with Login.gov — CalPrivacy suggests the Login.gov route only if you already have a Login.gov account.

Submitting for someone else. CalPrivacy states that in some cases you may submit a DROP request for another California resident — their examples are a parent for a child, or a family member for an elderly relative. The exact conditions are on their eligibility page; we haven't verified anything about commercial or professional authorized-agent submissions, so we won't claim it either way.

4 The three steps, on the state's site

From here you're on an official California government site. Nullecho does not fill anything in, does not watch what you type, and never sees your submission. Automating a verified-identity government process would risk invalidating your request — so this is a guided handoff, and that's deliberate.

  1. Verify your eligibility

    Confirm California residency via the California Identity Gateway. No account needed.

  2. Create your profile

    Enter as much or as little as you want — name, DOB and ZIP at minimum. Brokers compare this against their records; a match means deletion.

  3. Submit, and save your DROP ID

    You'll get an 8-digit DROP ID on the confirmation page. Save it somewhere safe — it's how you check status later. CalPrivacy: do not share your DROP ID.

    Nullecho deliberately does not offer to store your DROP ID. It's a credential for your deletion record, and a privacy tool has no business holding one. Put it in your password manager.

5 Record that you submitted

Local to this browser, on this machine. A date and nothing else — no name, no DROP ID, no identifiers. It exists so Nullecho can tell you when a status check is actually worth your time.

DROP request recorded

Why there's no "renew" reminder. We checked, because it would have been an easy thing to get wrong. CalPrivacy's wording is explicit: "DROP is ongoing – not a one-time action." After the first pass, brokers must re-check and delete newly matching data at least every 45 days on their own. Nothing in CalPrivacy's consumer guidance describes an expiry or a resubmission step.

So the dates above are check-ins, not renewals: the first is when status data should actually exist, and after that it's only worth returning when your own details change — a new name, phone, email, device (MAID) or car (VIN) gives brokers new things to match against, and you add those to the same profile.
