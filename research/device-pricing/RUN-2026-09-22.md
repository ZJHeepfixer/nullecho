# Device-price probe — run of 2026-09-22

Instrument `harness/device-price-probe.mjs` · sites `harness/device-price-sites.json` · raw per-load data
`raw/2026-09-22.json` · method, controls and limits in `METHOD.md` (read it before quoting anything below).

**In one paragraph.** Seventeen public pages — two hotel/lodging, two flight, one car-rental, nine retail,
three ticketing — were each loaded twelve times as four internally consistent device profiles (Chrome on
macOS, Chrome on Windows, Safari on an iPhone, Chrome on a low-end Android phone), interleaved, from one
connection, each load in a fresh incognito context, over 41 minutes. The declared price was read from
JSON-LD, microdata, meta or (four sites) a per-site visible-text selector. Result: **9 sites read the same
price on every device in every load; 6 were inconclusive; 2 were blocked; 0 showed a device-based price
difference that survived inspection.** One page (apple.com's iPad page) was read as "differs by device" by
the first-candidate rule in both the primary run and a re-run ten minutes later — the phone template lists
the page's 24 configurations in a different order than the desktop template does. A full comparison of
all 24 (name|sku → price) found every configuration priced identically on all four devices. It is recorded
as inconclusive, and the rule that caught it is now part of the probe.

## What this run does NOT show

- **A device difference is not proof of personal-data pricing.** Every visitor here was anonymous: no
  account, no cookie, no history. A site can quote by device class for reasons that involve no
  personal data (a mobile-only promotion, a different fee bundle, a different default variant).
- **One connection, one day, one region.** One residential connection in California, 13:58–14:39 Pacific
  on 2026-09-22. Nothing here generalises to another IP, another state, another week.
- **Anonymous visitors only.** Logged-in, loyalty, cart and checkout pricing were not tested — and are
  where personalisation would most plausibly live.
- **A price declared in markup may differ from what a person sees.** JSON-LD / meta prices are written
  for search engines; they are often right, sometimes stale, sometimes a base variant's — and on a
  multi-offer page the *first* one is whichever the template happens to list first (see Apple, below).
  Where a visible-text selector was used (flagged `visible`) the number is what rendered, parsed from text.
- **A/B experiments exist.** The same-device repeat check catches a bucket that flips inside our 12
  loads; it cannot catch one assigned once per connection.
- **Mobile sites may quote different fees or bundles.** A lower declared base price on a phone page
  can mean fees folded differently, not a lower total (Kayak's phone template shows a per-day rate first).
- **The engine under every profile is Chromium.** The "iPhone Safari" row is Chromium wearing Safari's
  UA, viewport, DPR and touch; consistent on the wire, but JavaScript feature-detection sees Chromium.
- **Automation is visible.** `navigator.webdriver` was `true` on every load. A site that quietly changed
  its behaviour for suspected automation would look identical to one that did nothing.
- **Phone coverage was thin exactly where it matters most.** On the two lodging pages the phone profiles
  rendered no price within the budget, and one electronics retailer refused the phone profiles at the
  connection level, so for hotels/lodging this run says nothing about device pricing either way.

`same` below means "did not vary, for these four devices, in these 12 loads, at this hour" — not "never varies".

## Observations worth keeping (facts only)

- **apple.com iPad** — read 599 USD (desktops) vs 549 USD (phones) in all three primary rounds, and
  699 vs 549 in all three re-check rounds ten minutes later. The page declares 24 `Offer`s, one per
  configuration. The desktop template's order also changed between round 1 and round 3 and again by
  the re-check; the phone template's order did not. The `offers` attack (one load per device, every
  offer with its `sku`) found 24 distinct configurations, **0 priced differently on any device**, and the
  same six-price set on all four. Ruled inconclusive: an ordering difference, not a price difference.
  This is the run's most important methodological result and the reason METHOD.md §1.6 now has the
  multi-offer check.
- **bestbuy.com** — both desktop profiles loaded 6/6 and read 243.37 USD; both phone profiles failed 6/6
  with `net::ERR_HTTP2_PROTOCOL_ERROR` (the connection was reset before any document), interleaved,
  same connection, same three minutes. A device-dependent refusal at the transport level. Nothing here
  says why; no claim about price.
- **dell.com** — `mac-chrome` got Akamai "Access Denied … Reference #18.…" (HTTP 403) on rounds 1–2 and a
  DNS failure on round 3; `win-chrome` got the same 403 once (round 2) and read 799.99 USD twice; both
  phones read 799.99 USD 3/3. Ruled inconclusive because one device never returned a price twice.
- **walmart.com** — round 1 read 266.99 (microdata, no currency declared) on all four devices; rounds 2
  onward returned PerimeterX's "Robot or human? Activate and hold the button to confirm that you're
  human." on three consecutive loads → abandoned. Note: the vetting load of the same URL at 13:38
  Pacific read **125.00** via the same `itemprop="price"` path. The first `itemprop=price` element on
  that page is evidently not always the headline item; treat the Walmart numbers as unverified.
- **kayak.com cars (LAX, 2 days, sorted by price)** — `mac-chrome` read 184, 184, 44; `win-chrome` 44 × 3;
  both phones 43 × 3. Within-device variation on the first slot (a sponsored placement) → inconclusive,
  as predicted in the sites file. The phone template renders "$22 $43 total" where desktop renders
  "$44 Total for 2 days"; that one-dollar gap is noted and **not** claimed — the same-device control
  failed on this page.
- **agoda.com** — desktops 122 USD × 6 ("from USD 122"); phones rendered only a header shell within 20 s
  on all 6 loads. **airbnb.com** — desktops 154 USD on 2 of 6 loads ("$154 for 2 nights"), no price on
  the other 4 within ~20 s; phones 0 of 6; one Android load was answered HTTP 429 with a body reading
  "Error code: 503 Airbnb is temporarily unavailable". Both inconclusive; neither says anything about
  device pricing.
- **seatgeek.com** — HTTP 403 with an empty body on three consecutive loads → abandoned. The vetting
  load at 13:44 Pacific had returned 200 with 24 JSON-LD blocks.
- **skiplagged.com** — the lowest LAX–JFK fare read 134 at vetting (13:42), 139 at the four-device check
  (~13:55) and 139 on all 12 run loads (14:01–14:03): it moved over fifteen minutes and held for three.
  That is the case interleaving exists for, and here it produced a clean `same`.
- **google.com/travel/flights** — 169 USD on all 12 loads, read from the accessible fare label; the phone
  profiles rendered at 393/360 px and the same label.
- The seven markup-declared retail/ticketing pages that read `same` (IKEA 16.99, Nike 115, Zappos 65.00,
  Away 295.0, Brooklinen 135.15, Eventbrite 29.85, GetYourGuide 79) each returned the same JSON-LD
  candidate set on every load; none showed a hydration rewrite.

## Three caveats a reader should carry away

1. **The only "differs by device" in 202 loads was an artefact of reading the first JSON-LD offer on a
   multi-offer page.** Any future device difference read from markup must pass the `offers` attack
   before it is reported at all.
2. **This run is silent on hotels and lodging.** Both lodging pages rendered a rate only on desktop
   profiles within the budget; the OTA and chain hotel pages tried during vetting were walled, challenged
   or client-rendered without a declared price. A mobile-emulation run with a longer budget, or a real
   phone, is needed before saying anything about device pricing in that sector.
3. **Sites do behave differently by device — just not, here, in the declared price.** Best Buy reset the
   phone profiles' connections; Dell walled the Mac profile and not the phones; Walmart walled everyone
   after four loads. Those are the facts; the reasons are not visible from here.
