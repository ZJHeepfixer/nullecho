```
Hi,

Maryland's Protection From Predatory Pricing Act takes effect October 1 — eight days from
now — and some of what retailers are reading about it, including at least one law-firm
client alert and the AI answers of the major search engines, describes a second section,
Com. Law § 13-322, requiring any merchant that prices on personal data to display "THIS
PRICE WAS SET BY AN ALGORITHM OR BY USING YOUR PERSONAL DATA."

That section was struck by amendment before the bill passed. What Maryland actually
enacted is § 13-321: a ban on dynamic pricing by large food retailers and third-party
food delivery, with no wording mandated at all. Checkable in a browser (not curl — the
statute text is rendered client-side):
https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gcl&section=13-321&enactments=true&archived=false
returns the enacted § 13-321 text; swap in section=13-322 and the same endpoint returns
"File Not Found."

The practical angle for your readers: a general retailer putting up a Maryland banner is
complying with nothing — and in food retail, where the conduct is banned, a banner could
read as an admission rather than a cure. (My inference, not legal advice.)

I raised the same point in a public comment to the FTC (docket FTC-2026-1057). I build
an open-source privacy extension; I'm not a lawyer. Sourced brief, free, if useful.

Jason Luker
Independent developer, Nullecho (free, open-source)
https://nullecho.org/research/
```
